🗺️ The tool map

Every week, a new AI tool appears, promising to change your life. Most disappear within a few months, change their name, or get swallowed up by a bigger company. That’s why the right way to find your bearings isn’t to memorize brands; it’s to understand the categories. Each category handles a type of work, and the tools within it come and go.

At the center of the map are general-purpose assistants — ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. As of October 2026, all three can communicate through text and voice, read files and images, search the web, and generate images or code. Around them are specialized tools for images, video, voice, code, research, and automation. Often, a general-purpose assistant is enough; a specialized tool comes in when you need more quality or control for a particular type of work.

What it is

The tool map divides the AI market into work categories: a general-purpose assistant at the center, with specialized tools around it, each suited to a particular type of output.

CategoryWell-known examplesWhen to move beyond the general-purpose assistant
ImageMidjourney, Ideogram, FLUX, Stable DiffusionWhen you need controlled styles or many variations (Module 1.5).
VideoRunway, Kling, Veo, HeyGenClips, image animation, talking avatars.
Voice and audioWhisper, ElevenLabs, SunoTranscribe long meetings, narrate, create a soundtrack.
CodeClaude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub CopilotWhen your project has multiple files and needs to run.
ResearchPerplexity, assistants’ in-depth research modesWhen you need cited sources to check.
Automationn8n, Make, ZapierWhen the same task repeats every week (Module 1.7).

💡 Tip

Names, plans, and prices change all the time, so this module doesn’t include a price table. Before you subscribe to anything, open the tool’s official page and check its current price. The selection method you’ll learn still works when the brands change.

Why learn

When you think in categories, you won’t feel lost with every new release. Look at the new tool and ask: which category does it fit into, and does it do the job better than what I already use? This saves money on subscriptions and time learning a tool that may disappear.

🎯 Choose with care

There’s no “best AI.” There’s the best one for your task, with your budget and your privacy requirements. Online rankings measure generic tests; what matters is how the tool performs on the text you write, the spreadsheet you analyze, and the customer you serve.

The most reliable approach is a side-by-side test: the same request, with the same material, in two or three tools on their free plans. Compare the answers against your own standards — and decide based on evidence, not advertising.

What it is

Choosing with care means evaluating tools with five questions — task, quality, cost, privacy, and integration — using a test based on your own work.

CriterionQuestion to askWarning sign
TaskWhat kind of output do I need: text, image, audio, or code?Choosing the tool before knowing the task.
QualityDoes it work well on MY material, in Portuguese?Deciding based only on a demo video.
CostIs the free plan enough? How much does it cost per month and per use?Subscribing to several tools that do the same thing.
PrivacyIs the data I send used for training? Can I turn that off?Terms of use you’ve never opened.
IntegrationDoes it work with what I already use (email, Drive, calendar)?Copying and pasting between five tabs all day.

Goal: compare two or three assistants on your actual task.

I’m going to use you for a task from my job and compare you with other assistants.
My role: <e.g., dental clinic receptionist>
The task: <e.g., reply to patients who ask to reschedule an appointment>
Sample material (no real names): <paste 2 or 3 typical messages>

1. Do the task for each example.
2. Tell me what else you’d need to know to do a better job.
3. Point out a common risk or mistake for this type of task.

How to check: paste the same request into each tool in a new conversation. Score three items from 1 to 5: got the tone right, followed the format, didn’t make anything up. The tool with the highest score on YOUR material is your choice for this task.

✓ Choose wisely

  • ✓Try the free plan before paying.
  • ✓Use a real (anonymized) example in your test.
  • ✓Reevaluate every six months: the leader changes.

✗ Common pitfalls

  • ✗Subscribing based on an ad without testing it on your material.
  • ✗Switching tools every week and never mastering any of them.
  • ✗Forgetting active subscriptions you no longer use.

Why learn

A choice backed by a real test can serve you for months. You stop paying for promises, gain confidence in the tool you use, and can explain to your boss or business partner why you chose it—with examples, not opinions.

Key concepts

Side-by-side test
same request, multiple AIs
Your own scoring guide
score it on your material
Free plan
where every test starts
Integration

🔑 Accounts and secure access

Your AI account holds much more than you might think: entire conversations, files you’ve uploaded, memories of what you’ve told it about yourself, and, increasingly, access to your email, calendar, and Drive. Anyone who gets into your account can see all of that. That’s why your assistant account deserves the same care as your bank account.

🌱 New here?

Password manager is an app that creates and stores a different, strong password for each site; you only have to remember one. Two-step verification (2FA) asks for a code on your phone in addition to your password—someone who steals your password still can’t get in. Passkey replaces your password with unlocking your own device using your fingerprint or face. Business plan is the version for businesses, with a contract and stricter data rules.

What it is

Secure access means protecting your AI accounts in layers: a unique password, two-step verification, a plan suited to the type of data, and regular reviews of connected apps.

🆓 Free

Good for learning and personal tasks. Usage limits are lower, and with many services, conversations may be used to improve the model—check the setting in your account preferences.

⭐ Individual paid plan

More usage, stronger models, and features like projects and agents. It’s still a personal account: read the data rules before using it with customer information.

🏢 Team or business

A contract, centralized administration, and a commitment not to train on your data. This is the right choice when customer, patient, or employee data is involved.

💡 Tip

Keep your personal account separate from your work account. If you change jobs or your company switches tools, nothing gets mixed together—and you won’t have customer conversations in the account tied to your personal email.

Why learn

Scams that start with a leaked password are common, and AI accounts have become valuable targets because they hold so much information. Turning on two-step verification and choosing the right plan takes less than half an hour and closes the door attackers use most often.

Key concepts

Password manager
a strong password for every site
2FA
password + code on your phone
Passkey
sign in with your device
Business plan
contract for customer data

🗂️ Organized files and conversations

After a few weeks of using AI, your conversation list turns into a messy drawer: “New conversation,” “New conversation,” “Help with text.” That great reply from Tuesday disappears among a hundred others. Getting organized isn’t busywork: it’s how you reuse what has already worked.

Today’s assistants have projects (or similar workspaces): folders where you bring together conversations, reference files, and standing instructions on a topic. A conversation opened inside the “Store — customer service” project already knows the brand’s tone and return policy because those files are there.

What it is

Getting organized means giving each recurring topic its own project, naming each conversation to show what it helps you do, and saving important final results outside the chat.

Step 1 · List your topics. Three to five recurring topics: customer service, posts, finances, studies.

Step 2 · Create a project for each topic. Add reference documents and standing instructions to it.

Step 3 · Rename your conversations. A simple format: date + what it helps you do. Example: “2026-10 reply to delivery complaint”.

Step 4 · Take the result outside. Final text goes to Drive or the official document; the chat is a draft, not an archive.

Step 5 · Do a monthly cleanup. Delete conversations that contained sensitive data and archive what you no longer use.

✓ Names that work

  • ✓proposal-client-A_v3_2026-10-14.docx
  • ✓Project "Clinic — responses to patients"
  • ✓Conversation "product X video script — final"

✗ Names that get lost

  • ✗final document FINAL this is it.docx
  • ✗"New conversation" repeated thirty times
  • ✗Everything in the Downloads folder, with no folders at all

💡 Tip

When a conversation produces a request that worked really well, copy the request into a document called "My prompts." In Module 1.3, this document becomes your prompt library.

Why learn

Organization turns casual use into a system. With projects and clear names, you can reuse what you’ve already done, the AI starts each conversation with the right context, and you can find in seconds what used to take half an hour to locate.

Key concepts

Project
folder with files and instructions
Naming format
date + what it solves
Chat is a draft
the final version lives outside it
Monthly cleanup

🛡️ What never to paste into a chat

Everything you paste into an AI tool leaves your computer and goes to a company's server. Most of the time, that isn't a problem. But when the text contains other people's data — clients, patients, students, employees — you become legally responsible for what you do with it.

🌱 New here?

LGPD is Brazil's General Data Protection Law (Law 13,709/2018), which regulates how companies and professionals in Brazil handle information about people. Personal data is anything that identifies someone: name, CPF, phone number, email, address. Sensitive data is the most protected category: health, religion, racial origin, political opinion, sexual orientation, biometrics.

What it is

This is the rule for classifying what you're going to paste before you paste it: green means okay, yellow means anonymize it or use a plan with a contract, and red means never put it in a regular chat.

Goal: learn to anonymize a text before asking for help.

I'll show you an invented EXAMPLE text to learn how to anonymize.
Text: "Maria Souza, CPF 000.000.000-00, lives at 12 Rua das Flores,
called from (11) 90000-0000 to complain about the delay on order 4471."

1. List each piece of personal data that appears and name its category (name, document,
   address, contact, other).
2. Rewrite the text, replacing each piece of data with a placeholder: [CLIENT_A],
   [CPF], [ADDRESS], [PHONE], [ORDER].
3. Give me a 5-item checklist so I can anonymize texts from my work on my own before pasting them:
   <your field, e.g., retail customer service>.

How to check: Reread the rewritten text and look for anything that could still identify the person. Note that the order number could also identify someone within the company. Then use the checklist manually — ideally, you anonymize the text yourself before pasting it, instead of asking AI to do that with the real data.

✓ Safe habits

  • ✓Always anonymize before pasting.
  • ✓Turn off the use of conversations for training when the tool allows it.
  • ✓Ask the company which tool is approved.

✗ Mistakes that cause problems

  • ✗Pasting the entire client spreadsheet to "make a summary."
  • ✗Sending a photo of an ID or medical test in a free chat.
  • ✗Using your personal account with your employer's data.

Why learn

A customer data leak can lead to fines, lawsuits, and, above all, lost trust. The traffic light is a two-second habit that protects you, your company, and the people whose data passes through your hands.

personal data law
Sensitive data

Anonymizing

🔍 Check what the AI provides

In Module 1.1, you saw that AI can make things up with confidence. Now comes the practical part: a short checklist for verifying an answer before sending it to a client, publishing it, or making a decision. The more serious the consequences of an error, the more checks you apply.

The most powerful check is called lateral reading. Instead of rereading the answer to look for errors (the text always sounds convincing), leave it and open another source — the official website, the published law, the original document — to see if it matches.

What it is

Verification means checking the answer against four filters — source, numbers, consistency, and a second opinion — in proportion to the damage an error could cause.

Try this: ask the AI itself to point out what to check.

Review the answer you gave me above as a rigorous fact-checker.

1. List every claim I should verify before using it
   (numbers, dates, names, laws, prices, quotations).
2. For each one, tell me what kind of official source I should check
   (e.g., the agency’s website, the original document). Don’t invent links.
3. Give a confidence rating (high, medium, low) and explain why.
4. If anything may have changed since your cutoff date, say so.

How to check: Use this list as a guide, not as proof. Open the suggested source for at least the items with medium or low confidence. If the AI corrects itself during this review, that’s a sign the first answer had an error — good catch.

Why learn

Checking is what makes it possible to use AI for serious work. If you check, you deliver faster with the same confidence as before. If you don’t, you deliver quickly until the day of your first public mistake—and then you lose the credibility that took years to build.

Key concepts

Lateral reading
leave the text and check elsewhere
Original source
the document, not the summary
Second opinion
another AI, same question
Risk of error
determines how much to verify

🧰 Your minimal digital toolkit

Putting it all together: you don’t need ten subscriptions. A small, well-configured toolkit covers most tasks for someone just getting started. Add the rest when a real task calls for it — not when an ad pops up.

What it is

The minimum toolkit is one primary general-purpose assistant, one place to store files, a password manager, and a specialized tool only if a task calls for it.

🤖 1 primary assistant

ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — whichever won your side-by-side test. Keep a second, free one for a second opinion.

☁️ 1 place for files

Google Drive, OneDrive, or another option — with folders organized by topic. That’s where the final version lives.

🔐 1 password manager

Bitwarden, 1Password, or the one built into your browser — with two-step verification on your main accounts.

🎯 0 or 1 specialized tool

Only if a recurring task calls for one: image generation if you create posts, transcription if you record meetings.

Goal: build your minimum toolkit and a security plan in one conversation.

I want to build a minimum toolkit of digital tools with AI.
My job: <your role>
Tasks I do most often: <list 5>
Tools I already use: <email, Drive, WhatsApp Business, spreadsheets...>
Monthly budget for tools: <amount or "free only">
Do I handle client data? <yes/no, and what kind>

Create:
1. A table: tool | task it’s for | free or paid | why.
   No more than 4 tools. Don’t give me prices: I’ll check the official website.
2. A security checklist for me to complete today (password, two-step verification,
   privacy settings, separating accounts).
3. What kinds of data I should NOT paste in, considering LGPD (Brazil’s data protection law) and my field.

How to check: The toolkit has no more than four items, and each one is tied to one of your tasks. If the answer pushes tools you didn’t ask for, reply, "remove everything that isn’t tied to one of my 5 tasks." Complete the security checklist the same day.

💡 Tip

Schedule a review every six months: which tools you actually used, which subscriptions to cancel, and whether your primary assistant still wins the side-by-side test.

Why learn

A few tools you know well do more for you than many tools you only partly use. The essential toolkit keeps your money, attention, and data under control—and gives the next modules a foundation to build on.
