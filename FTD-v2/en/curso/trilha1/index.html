🧠 The Impact of AI

What artificial intelligence really is, where it came from, what it changes at work, and where it gets things wrong.

What it is:

AI is a broad field. Within it is machine learning, which learns from data; within machine learning is deep learning, which uses neural networks with many layers; and within deep learning are the language models you use every day.

Why learn it:

When you understand that AI is a pattern recognizer, you know when to trust it and when to check its work. If you think it’s magic, you swing between fear and blind trust—and both can be costly.

Key concepts:

Model patterns stored as numbers Training learning from examples Deep learning networks with many layers Pattern something that repeats in data

What it is:

What it is: AI history is a series of leaps: from hand-written rules, to learning from data, to deep neural networks, to language models, and now to agents that perform tasks.

Why learn it:

The timeline shows that today’s leap came from three things coming together: lots of data, lots of computing power, and a better architecture (the Transformer). Knowing this helps you anticipate what’s next and separate real progress from hype.

Key concepts:

AI winter a promise that never came true Expert system hand-written rules Transformer the foundation of LLMs Agent AI that performs tasks

What it is:

What it is: An LLM is a model trained on a huge amount of text to predict the next token. It then goes through a second round of training with examples of good answers and human feedback, turning it into an assistant that follows instructions.

Why learn it:

Understanding that the model predicts text explains almost everything you’ll see: why context improves the answer, why it confidently makes things up, and why asking for a clear format works. It’s the mental model that guides the rest of the course.

Key concepts:

Token piece of text Context window what fits at once Probability the chance of each continuation Pretraining learning to complete text

What it is:

What it is: Generative AI produces text, images, audio, video, and code from a prompt. Each type of output has its own tools and considerations, but the principle is the same: the clearer the prompt, the better the result.

Why learn it:

Knowing what’s already possible helps you avoid two mistakes: spending hours doing by hand what AI can do in minutes, and expecting it to do things it still can’t do well. This is the map you’ll explore in Modules 1.3 through 1.7.

Key concepts:

Generative AI creates new content Multimodal several types of content Prompt the written request Output what the model delivers

What it is:

What it is: The change is about who does each task: AI takes on drafting, searching, formatting, and repetition; people handle direction, verification, decisions, and relationships.

Why learn it:

Looking at tasks takes away the abstract fear (“Will I lose my job?”) and brings up a practical question: which of my tasks can AI already do well, and what will I do with the time I save? The people who answer this first get ahead.

Key concepts:

Task × job AI enters through tasks Delegation increases step by step Verification increases with autonomy Judgment what stays with you

What it is:

What it is: These are AI’s known limitations: making up facts (hallucinations), not knowing what happened after its training cutoff date, repeating biases in its training data, and exposing what you paste into the conversation (privacy).

Why learn it:

Using AI without knowing its limits is like driving without knowing where the brakes are. The habit of checking facts, anonymizing data, and questioning overly confident answers is what separates people who use AI safely from those who embarrass themselves.

Key concepts:

Hallucination a false claim made with confidence Cutoff date the limit of the training data Bias inherited prejudice Anonymize protect data before pasting

What it is:

What it is: A guided exercise: you describe your routine, AI organizes the tasks by their potential for AI use, and you choose one to test this week.

Why learn it:

Just reading about AI doesn’t make what you learn stick. Using it on one of your real tasks, with a short test and a check, turns a concept into a habit—and shows you in practice what this module explained.

Key concepts:

Task map where AI fits Short test 15 minutes, a real task Follow-up question AI asks before acting Effort × gain where to start

🧭 Navigating the Digital World

Choose tools, organize accounts and files, protect data, and evaluate what AI produces.

What it is:

What it is: The tool map divides the AI market into work categories: a general assistant at the center and specialized tools around it, each suited to a type of output.

Why learn it:

When you think in categories, you won’t feel lost with every new release. Look at the new tool and ask: which category does it fit into, and does it do the job better than what I already use? This saves money on subscriptions and time learning a tool that may disappear.

Key concepts:

General assistant the center of the map Specialized tool one type of output, more control Category lasts longer than the brand Official page source for today’s price

What it is:

What it is: Choosing carefully means evaluating tools with five questions—task, quality, cost, privacy, and integration—and testing one with your own work.

Why learn it:

A choice backed by a real test can serve you for months. You stop paying for promises, gain confidence in the tool you use, and can explain to your boss or business partner why you chose it—with examples, not opinions.

Key concepts:

Side-by-side test, same prompt, multiple AIs Your own scoring rubric for your materials Free plan where every test starts Integration that works with the tools you use

What it is:

What it is: Secure access means protecting your AI accounts in layers: a unique password, two-step verification, a plan suited to the type of data, and regular reviews of connected apps.

Why learn it:

Scams that start with a leaked password are common, and AI accounts have become valuable targets because they hold so much information. Turning on two-step verification and choosing the right plan takes less than half an hour and closes the door attackers use most often.

Key concepts:

Password manager A strong password for each site 2FA Password + code on your phone Passkey Sign in with your device Business plan Contract covering customer data

What it is:

What it is: Organizing means giving each recurring subject a project, naming each conversation to show what it helps you solve, and keeping important final results outside the chat.

Why learn it:

Organization turns casual use into a system. With projects and clear names, you can reuse what you’ve already done, the AI starts each conversation with the right context, and you can find in seconds what used to take half an hour to locate.

Key concepts:

Project Folder with files and instructions Naming convention Date + what it helps you solve Chat is a draft The final version lives outside it Monthly cleanup Delete anything sensitive

What it is:

What it is: It’s a rule for classifying what you’re about to paste before you paste it: green means okay, yellow means only after anonymizing or on a plan with a contract, and red means never put it in a regular chat.

Why learn it:

A customer data leak can lead to fines, lawsuits, and, above all, lost trust. The traffic light is a two-second habit that protects you, your company, and the people whose data passes through your hands.

Key concepts:

LGPD Personal data law Sensitive data Health, religion, biometrics Anonymize Replace data with placeholders Traffic light Classify before pasting

What it is:

What it is: Checking means running the answer through four filters—sources, numbers, consistency, and a second opinion—in proportion to the damage an error could cause.

Why learn it:

Checking is what makes it possible to use AI for serious work. If you check, you deliver faster with the same confidence as before. If you don’t, you deliver quickly until the day of your first public mistake—and then you lose the credibility that took years to build.

Key concepts:

Lateral reading Leave the text and check elsewhere Original source The document, not the summary Second opinion Another AI, same question Risk of error Determines how much to check

What it is:

What it is: The essential toolkit includes one main general-purpose assistant, a place to store files, a password manager, and a specialized tool only if a task calls for it.

Why learn it:

A few tools you know well do more for you than many tools you only partly use. The essential toolkit keeps your money, attention, and data under control—and gives the next modules a foundation to build on.

Key concepts:

Essential toolkit Up to 4 tools Main assistant The winner of your test Security checklist Completed the same day Review every six months Cut what you don’t use

✍️ Prompt Engineering

The anatomy of a good prompt, examples, output format, iteration, and reusable prompt templates.

What it is:

What it is: Prompt engineering means writing a prompt so there’s little doubt about what you want: who it’s for, what it’s for, what format you want, and what limits apply.

Why learn it:

The same assistant seems “dumb” to someone who writes a poor prompt and “brilliant” to someone who writes a good one. Writing a complete prompt takes one extra minute and saves several rounds of “that’s not what I meant.” It’s the skill that most improves your results with AI, in any tool.

Key concepts:

Prompt The complete request Context Narrows the range of answers Generic answer A symptom of a vague prompt Output What the AI gives you

What it is:

What it is: The anatomy of a prompt is a checklist of six parts—role, context, task, examples, format, and constraints—that you use to put together complete prompts.

Why learn it:

Once you know the anatomy, you can stop relying on “magic prompts” copied from the internet. You can build your own for your situation, and diagnose what’s missing when the answer falls short: it’s almost always context, format, or a constraint.

Key concepts:

Role Adjusts tone and focus Context The part that matters most Task Clear verb, one at a time Constraint What to leave out

What it is:

What it is: Using examples means including a few models of the expected result in your prompt, so the AI can copy the pattern instead of guessing what you meant by vague words.

Why learn it:

Examples solve in a second what ten lines of description can’t. It’s the fastest way to get the AI to write “your way,” follow your company’s standards, or classify things exactly as you would.

Key concepts:

Zero-shot Prompt without an example Few-shot A few examples first Pattern What the AI imitates Reference Attached document

What it is:

What it is Output format is the form of the response — structure, length, tone, and language — set in the request itself, according to where the text will be used.

Why learn it:

Asking for the right format turns the AI’s response into something you can use as is, without rework. It’s the detail that separates “the AI gave me some text” from “the AI gave me the table ready to paste into the spreadsheet.”

Key concepts:

Destination determines the format Concrete number works better than an adjective Formal, approachable, technical tone Conclusion first, before the details

What it is:

What it is Iterating means improving the response in short rounds: ask, review with clear criteria, give specific feedback, and ask again until you get a version you’d use.

Why learn it:

If you expect perfection in the first response, you’ll get frustrated and give up. If you treat the first response as a draft and know how to give feedback, you’ll get a quality result in a few rounds — and learn with each round what to include in your next request.

Key concepts:

Round ask, review, adjust Specific feedback what to change and where Questions first the AI asks what’s missing Self-critique the AI points out weaknesses

What it is:

What it is These are techniques for complex requests: break the task into connected steps, keep the response grounded in the material you provide, and enable extended reasoning when the task requires logic.

Why learn it:

Difficult tasks are exactly where you save the most time when things go well — and where things cause the most trouble when they go wrong. Breaking down, grounding, and reasoning let you use AI for serious work, with checkpoints along the way.

Key concepts:

Connected steps one task at a time Grounding only what’s in the material Reasoning mode think before responding Quote the passage proof for each claim

What it is:

What it is A prompt library is an organized collection of requests that worked, written as templates with variables, ready to copy, fill in, and use.

Why learn it:

A library turns one good response into a repeatable result. It’s also easy to share with your team: everyone starts asking in the same way, and quality no longer depends on who’s typing.

Key concepts:

Prompt template request with variables Variable what changes, between < > Fixed instructions apply in every conversation Test with a new case proves the template works

🤖 AI Assistants and Agents

The difference between talking with an assistant and delegating to an agent, with tools, memory, and limits.

What it is:

What it is An assistant is AI that talks with you and provides text for you to act on. An agent is AI that receives an objective and acts on its own, using tools in multiple steps until it finishes or asks for help.

Why learn it:

Knowing the difference helps you avoid two kinds of waste: doing step by step what an agent could do on its own, and giving an agent a delicate task that called for your control, sentence by sentence. The right choice depends on the task, not the trend.

Key concepts:

Assistant responds; you act Agent receives an objective and executes Autonomy how much it acts without you Supervision increases with autonomy

What it is:

What it is Customizing means saving who you are, how you want responses, and reference material in the assistant, at three levels: general instructions, projects by topic, and assistants dedicated to a specific function.

Why learn it:

Customization turns a generic assistant into a collaborator that knows your context. Every conversation starts off better, you type less, and quality stays consistent — including when other people on your team use the same dedicated assistant.

Key concepts:

General instructions apply in every conversation Project files + rules for a topic GPT / Gem assistant for one function Reference material what the AI consults

What it is:

What it is Tools and connectors are extensions that let AI search for current information, read and create files, do calculations, browse, and access your services — always with permissions you grant.

Why learn it:

Tools address limitations you saw in Module 1.1: search gets around the knowledge cutoff, code prevents calculation errors, and files ground the response. Knowing which ones to turn on — and which to leave off — is what makes AI useful and safe at the same time.

Key concepts:

Tool action outside the text Connector connection to your services MCP universal outlet for tools Read × write writing calls for more care

What it is:

What it is The agent cycle is to plan, act with a tool, observe the result, and adjust, repeating until it reaches the objective, with checkpoints where it stops and asks for your approval.

Why learn it:

Understanding the cycle makes it clear where an agent can go wrong — in a search step, a reading, or a decision — and where you should add checkpoints. When you understand the loop, you write better work orders and trust the result for the right reasons.

Key concepts:

Goal or outcome, not the steps Agent loop plan, act, observe, adjust Human approval before irreversible actions Work order goal + limits + stopping point

What it is:

What it is Memory is a set of layers that carry information from one conversation to another: fixed instructions, project files, and automatic notes about you, all visible and editable in settings.

Why learn it:

Well-maintained memory helps the assistant get more right with less explanation. Neglected memory can bring sensitive data into conversations where it doesn’t belong, or make AI act on outdated information. You control this—but only if you know where to look.

Key concepts:

Memory facts carried across conversations Temporary chat doesn’t add to memory Context window limit of one conversation Summary and restart a long conversation becomes a new one

What it is:

What it is Agent safety means limiting what agents can access and do, requiring approval before irreversible actions, treating instructions from external content with suspicion, and checking the result at the end.

Why learn it:

Agents will become more capable and more present in the workplace. If you learn now to grant minimal permissions, approve irreversible actions, and recognize prompt injection, you can save time without risking an email being sent in your name by mistake.

Key concepts:

Least privilege only the access needed Irreversible action send, pay, delete Prompt injection hidden instruction in content Revoke access turn off what you don’t use

What it is:

What it is A dedicated assistant is a setup with a single purpose, instructions written using the six parts from module 1.3, reference material attached, and a five-question test before real use.

Why learn it:

A well-tested dedicated assistant is the bridge between “I use AI once in a while” and “AI is part of my process.” It’s also the first step toward the automation in module 1.7: what works here, with clear rules, can later run on its own.

Key concepts:

Single purpose one function, done well Always / Never explicit rules 5-question test common case, detail, out of scope, trap, injection Turn errors into rules continuous improvement

🎨 Visual Creativity with AI

Generate and edit images and videos with intention: composition, style, consistency, and usage rights.

What it is:

What it is AI image generation creates a new image from text (or a reference image). The model doesn’t copy an existing photo: it assembles pixels that match the patterns it learned and what you asked for.

Why learn it:

Knowing that an image emerges from noise guided by text explains why the same sentence produces different results, why fine details fail, and why a well-described request saves attempts. You stop “leaving it to chance” and start directing.

Key concepts:

Diffusion remove noise until it becomes an image Visual prompt the description that guides it Generation new image, not a search Gap what you don’t say, the model makes up

What it is:

What it is The anatomy of a visual prompt is a fixed sequence of six layers—subject, setting, style, lighting, composition, and format with constraints—that covers what the model needs to know so it won’t fill in the gaps on its own.

Why learn it:

A fixed six-layer outline works with any tool, now and in the next version. It reduces attempts, makes results more predictable, and can also help you explain the idea to a human designer.

Key concepts:

Subject who or what appears Composition angle and framing Aspect ratio 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9 Constraints what must not appear

What it is:

What it is It’s a map of four visual tasks—create from scratch, edit with instructions, transform one image into another, and generate locally—with familiar tool examples for each. Names and prices change; the tasks stay the same.

Why learn it:

Choosing by task helps you avoid subscribing to three services that do the same thing and avoids forcing a tool to do something it does poorly. When a new generator appears, you’ll know which row of the table it belongs in.

Key concepts:

Text → image create from scratch Edit with instructions make a change with one sentence Open model runs on your computer License what’s allowed

What it is:

What it is Consistency means keeping the character, product, style, and color palette the same across multiple images. You can do this with a fixed reference image, a repeated base description, and targeted edits instead of generating everything again.

Why learn it:

A brand is recognized through repetition: the same colors, the same style, the same character. Without consistency, AI generates disconnected assets; with it, you can build a visual identity that lasts for months and that your audience recognizes from a distance.

Key concepts:

Reference image that anchors the look Base text repeated description Inpainting edit only one area Upscale enlarge with detail

What it is:

What AI video is: generating clips from text or an initial image, creating narration with a synthetic voice, music and effects, and putting everything together in an editor. It also includes talking avatars that read a script as a virtual person.

Why learn it:

Short video is the format that circulates most on social media, and making one used to require a camera, a crew, and editing. With the workflow script → keyframe → clip → assembly, one person can make a decent piece—provided they direct each step.

Key concepts:

Clip short generated scene Keyframe image that starts the scene Avatar virtual person who speaks Assembly putting clips, voice, and music together

What it is:

What it is: A set of precautions for using generated images and videos without violating others’ rights: follow terms of use and licenses, don’t use someone’s face or voice without permission, avoid other people’s brands, and disclose when content is synthetic.

Why learn it:

A piece that violates image rights or misleads the public can cost far more than it saved: removal, a lawsuit, and loss of trust. These precautions take minutes and protect you and your brand.

Key concepts:

Terms of use what the tool allows Image rights face and voice require permission Label disclose that it’s generated LGPD a face is also personal data

What it is:

What it is: A complete exercise: you write a short brief, ask the assistant for three different visual prompts, generate the images, choose one, make any needed corrections, and go through a review checklist before publishing.

Why learn it:

The brief and review are what separate a generic image from a piece that communicates. Completing the whole cycle once with one of your own pieces helps you learn the method and gives you a model for all the next ones.

Key concepts:

Brief the request before the prompt Visual direction a style choice Review a critical look before publishing Message understood in 3 seconds

📣 AI Marketing and Sales

Audience research, a content calendar, copy, customer service, and measurement using AI without losing your voice.

What it is:

What it is: AI-powered audience research means gathering what customers have already said (in reviews, messages, and comments) and asking AI to group their pain points, desires, objections, and the expressions they use. The result becomes the foundation for all your content.

Why learn it:

Content that uses the customer’s own words feels like it was written for them, which is why it works. Without research, AI produces copy that could fit any business—and for that very reason, catches no one’s attention.

Key concepts:

Persona customer profile Pain point the problem that drives them Objection what makes them hesitate Customer’s voice their own words

What it is:

What it is: A voice guide is a short text with the tone, vocabulary, examples of right and wrong phrasing, and brand rules. Saved as a permanent instruction in the assistant, it makes every new piece of writing sound like you.

Why learn it:

Your voice is what helps customers recognize your brand without seeing the logo. A saved guide keeps AI fast without erasing that identity, and also helps any new team member write in the company’s style.

Key concepts:

Voice guide how the brand speaks Tone 3 adjectives with examples Permanent instruction read in every conversation Blind test would someone recognize the brand?

What it is:

What it is: A content calendar is a schedule of what you’ll publish, on which day, in which format, and for what purpose. It’s built around three or four topic pillars that rotate throughout the week.

Why learn it:

Consistency is what helps your audience remember you. A calendar with pillars takes away the stress of “What do I post today?” and keeps AI working within a strategy instead of producing volume without direction.

Key concepts:

Pillar recurring topic Pace what you can sustain Format carousel, video, text Repurpose one post, multiple channels

What it is:

What it is: Writing sales copy with a simple structure—hook, problem, solution, proof, and call to action—using AI to draft and create variations, and you to make sure everything written is true.

Why learn it:

A clear structure helps short copy sell more than long, confusing copy. And honesty sells for longer: a misled customer won’t come back and will tell others. AI speeds up the writing; you build trust.

Key concepts:

Copy text that prompts action Hook the first line Proof always real Call to action one specific action

What it is:

What it is: Using AI at every stage of the funnel—attract, engage, talk, sell, and bring customers back—with clearly defined tasks: draft a reply, build an FAQ, prepare a proposal, and send a reminder. A person sends and makes the decisions.

Why learn it:

Responding quickly and well, and remembering to follow up with someone who asked for a quote, often brings in more sales than posting more. This is where AI saves time without removing the human touch that closes a deal.

Key concepts:

Funnel from first contact to next purchase Lead interested prospect who hasn’t bought Follow-up agreed check-in Human in charge AI drafts, person sends

What it is:

What it is: Measuring means choosing three to five indicators tied to your goal, recording them every week in a simple spreadsheet, and asking AI to compare periods, point out what changed, and suggest a test for the following week.

Why learn it:

Without measuring, you repeat what takes effort and abandon what gets results without realizing it. With three or four of the right numbers and AI helping you interpret them, each month teaches you something concrete about your customer.

Key concepts:

Vanity metric looks good, but doesn’t sell Source of the sale which channel it came from Test change one thing at a time Pattern × chance did it happen again, or was it luck?

What it is:

What it is: A short, complete campaign for a single offer: one day to research, one to prepare your voice and calendar, four days to publish and engage, and the last day to measure and decide on the next step.

Why learn it:

Completing the whole cycle once, on a small scale and with good measurement, teaches you more than reading about marketing for months. And the cycle repeats: research, voice, content, engagement, and measurement become your AI-assisted sales routine.

Key concepts:

One focused offer per campaign Ready-made responses before publishing Cycle research, publish, measure Learning what carries over to the next one

⚙️ Process Automation

Map a process, choose the trigger, build the workflow, and let AI handle the right steps.

What it is:

What it is: Process automation means getting apps to communicate with each other so they can perform, without you, a sequence of steps that used to be manual. It always starts with a trigger and continues with actions, sometimes with decisions along the way.

Why learn it:

Small, repeated tasks add up to hours each month, and they’re where you’re most likely to forget things or make typing errors. Thinking in terms of “trigger and action” helps you spot these tasks in your routine—and is the first step toward taking them off your hands.

Key concepts:

Trigger the event that starts things Action what the workflow does Workflow trigger + actions in order Integration apps that communicate

What it is:

What it is: Mapping a process means describing every step of a repeated task—input, decisions, output, and exceptions—so you can choose what’s worth automating and design the workflow before building it in the tool.

Why learn it:

Half an hour of mapping can prevent days of rework in the tool. Plus, just writing down the process often helps you find unnecessary steps you can cut—sometimes the best automation is simply to stop doing something.

Key concepts:

Map the process on paper Exception when it deviates from the norm 3P method prioritize, plan, pilot Pilot run in parallel first

What it is:

What it is: These are visual platforms where each workflow step is a block (called a step, module, or node) connected to the next. You choose the app, connect your account, and specify which data passes from one block to another.

Why learn it:

Knowing how the tools differ helps you avoid choosing based on advertising. For most first workflows, any of them will work; the choice matters when volume, sensitive data, or a desire to run everything on your own server comes into play.

Key concepts:

Node / module / step one workflow step Connector ready-made block for an app Credential access to your account Self-host run on your server

What it is:

What it is: A six-step workflow—form, AI, spreadsheet, decision, notification, and draft—that turns each quote request into an organized record and a reply ready for review, without sending anything on its own.

Why learn it:

Seeing a complete workflow, with a decision and human review, shows the pattern repeated in almost every useful automation: input, AI processing, record, rule, and supervised output. Once you build this one, you can adapt it for customer service, finance, or HR.

Key concepts:

AI node the model inside the workflow JSON response in fixed fields IF node splits the path Draft prepares, doesn’t send

What it is:

What it is: It’s the step-by-step choice between fixed rules and AI. AI handles classification, data extraction, summarizing, and drafting text; fixed rules handle copying, comparing, calculating, and sending. When an error could be costly, a person approves it.

Why learn it:

Putting AI in every step makes the workflow more expensive, slower, and less predictable. Using it only where needed—and requiring human approval where errors matter—gives you the best of both worlds: the machine reads, the rule executes, and the person decides.

Key concepts:

Fixed rule same result every time Classify label the text Extract pull data from the text Human approval where errors matter

What it is:

What it is: These are the safeguards that make automation reliable: test with fake data, get an alert when something goes wrong, check the execution history, limit AI costs, and handle personal data in accordance with the LGPD (Brazil’s data protection law).

Why learn it:

An automation without monitoring becomes a hidden risk: it can stop working, send the wrong message, or expose data. These safeguards take little time and make it possible to trust the workflow and let it run for real.

Key concepts:

Test data experiment without risk Error flow alerts you when something breaks History what each run did Minimal data only what is needed (LGPD)

What it is:

What it is An exercise to choose a simple automation, ask the assistant for the complete workflow specification, and build it in the tool you chose, following the module’s method.

Why learn it:

Getting your first automation working changes how you see work: every repeated task becomes a candidate. Starting small helps make that first experience a success instead of a shock.

Key concepts:

Low risk easy to fix errors Specification the workflow described in advance Expected result what the test should produce Inventory a list of your workflows

🚀 Advanced Artificial Intelligence

Context windows, your own documents, reasoning models, multimodality, and local AI, explained without jargon.

What it is:

What it is A context window is the maximum amount of text a model considers at one time in a conversation—the question, history, files, and the response itself, all added together. It works like a person’s working memory: large, but finite.

Why learn it:

Many of the poor answers people blame on “dumb AI” come from confusing conversations: mixed topics, rules forgotten outside the window, too many files. Understanding the window helps you organize your work into clean conversations, and quality improves without…

Key concepts:

Context window what fits at once Token the unit of the window Clean conversation one topic per conversation Context packet a summary to start again

What it is:

What it is It means having AI consult your files before answering: the system divides documents into sections, finds the ones related to the question, and gives those sections to the model along with the question. The answer is then based on what’s yours, with…

Why learn it:

This is the use that saves the most time in everyday work: finding a clause, answering a customer’s question with the right policy, studying a public notice. It also reduces fabrication the most, because the answer can point to its source. Before pasting personal data from…

Key concepts:
