🔎 Know your audience

All good marketing starts before the first post: it starts with knowing who buys, why they buy, and what words they use to describe their problem. AI is great at this when you give it real material—comments, reviews, questions that come in on WhatsApp—and ask it to organize the patterns. When you give it nothing, it invents a generic customer.

🌱 New here?

Target audience is the group of people you want to serve. Persona is a portrait of that group as if it were one person, with a routine, pain points, and wants. Pain point is a problem that bothers a customer enough to make them look for a solution. Objection is the reason they hesitate when it’s time to buy (“it’s expensive,” “I don’t know if it will work for me”).

What it is

AI-powered audience research means gathering what customers have already said (in reviews, messages, and comments) and asking AI to group their pain points, wants, objections, and the phrases they use. The result becomes the foundation for all your content.

Goal: build a customer map from what they’ve already written.

You are a market research analyst.
My business: <what you sell and who you sell it to, in one sentence>
Below are real comments, reviews, and questions from customers
(I’ve already removed names, phone numbers, and email addresses):
---
<paste 20 to 50 excerpts, one per line>
---
1. Group them into: pain points, wants, objections, and where and when they shop.
2. For each group, quote the exact phrases customers used.
3. Say which groups appear most often and which appear less often, without making up percentages.
4. List 5 questions I should ask customers to fill in what’s missing.

How to check: Look for three of the phrases quoted in the original material. If any phrase “in quotation marks” isn’t there, the AI made it up—ask it to try again using only exact excerpts. Remember: customer data is personal information under the LGPD (Brazil’s data protection law); remove names and contact details before pasting it.

Why learn

Content that uses the customer’s own words feels like it was written for them, which is why it works. Without research, AI produces copy that could fit any business—and for that very reason, catches no one’s attention.

🗣️ Your brand voice

The biggest risk of using AI in marketing is that every text ends up sounding the same: enthusiastic, full of adjectives, and “written by a robot.” The solution is to write a voice guide once—how your brand speaks, which words it uses and avoids—and keep that guide saved for the AI to consult with every request.

What it is

A voice guide is a short text that describes your tone, vocabulary, examples of phrases to use and avoid, and your brand rules. Save it as a standing instruction for your assistant, and each new text will sound like you.

✓ Sounds human

  • ✓“We deliver within 3 business days in the capital. Outside the capital, we let you know the delivery time in advance.”
  • ✓Short sentences, “you,” concrete examples.
  • ✓One idea per post, expressed clearly.

✗ Sounds robotic

  • ✗“Discover the revolutionary experience that will transform your life!”
  • ✗Too many emojis, exclamation points, and hashtags.
  • ✗Vague promises that don’t say what the customer gets.

🌱 New here?

Standing instruction It’s a text the assistant reads before every conversation. Each tool gives it a different name: “Projects” and “custom instructions” in ChatGPT, “Projects” in Claude, and “Gems” in Gemini. The idea is the same: you write it once and don’t have to repeat yourself.

Goal: extract the brand’s voice guide from texts you’ve already approved.

Here are 3 to 6 texts that represent how my brand speaks
(posts, emails, replies to customers that I liked):
---
<paste the texts>
---
Write a voice guide no longer than 1 page with:
1. Tone in 3 adjectives, each with an “this, not that” example.
2. Words and expressions we use and avoid.
3. Formatting rules (sentence length, emojis, exclamation points, hashtags).
4. Two examples of a good sentence and two of a bad sentence on the same subject.
5. One final rule: “when in doubt, choose clarity over enthusiasm.”
Write the guide so I can paste it in as a permanent instruction.

How to check: save the guide as a permanent instruction and ask for a post on any topic. Show the post to someone who knows the brand without telling them where it came from. If anything feels off to them, adjust the guide based on their feedback.

Why learn

Your voice is what helps customers recognize your brand without seeing the logo. A saved guide keeps AI fast without erasing that identity, and also helps any new team member write in the company’s style.

Key concepts

how the brand speaks
Tone

Standing instruction

Blind test

📅 A calendar you can stick to

AI can write thirty posts in minutes. The problem was never writing; it’s staying consistent and having something meaningful to say. A good calendar starts with a few pillars — the topics your brand keeps coming back to — and a pace you can maintain for months, not just one enthusiastic week.

What it is

A content calendar is a schedule of what you’ll publish, on which day, in what format, and for what purpose. It’s built around three to four topic pillars that rotate throughout the week.

PillarGoalExample (physical therapy clinic)
TeachAnswer a small question for free.“3 stretches for people who work sitting down.”
ShowProve that you do what you say.Behind the scenes of an evaluation, with the patient’s permission.
ConnectShow who’s behind the business.Why the physical therapist chose this profession.
InviteAsk for a specific action.“Free evaluation for the first 10 people who contact us on WhatsApp.”

💡 Tip

One good post can become several. Ask AI to turn a carousel into a short video script, an email, and a WhatsApp message. Repurposing with some adaptation goes further than creating from scratch every day.

Why learn

Consistency is what helps your audience remember you. A calendar with pillars takes away the stress of “What do I post today?” and keeps AI working within a strategy instead of producing volume without direction.

Key concepts

Pillar
recurring topic
Pace
what you can sustain
Format
carousel, video, text
Repurpose
one post, several channels

✍️ Copy that sells without overdoing it

Copy is writing designed to get someone to take action: click, reply, buy. Good copy isn’t the most enthusiastic; it’s what shows the customer you understand their problem and clearly explains what they’ll get. AI can help build the structure and create variations — you’re still responsible for making sure the promise is true.

What it is

It means writing sales copy with a simple structure — hook, problem, solution, proof, and call to action — using AI to draft and create variations, while you make sure everything written is true.

✓ Honest copy

  • ✓Says exactly what the customer gets and when.
  • ✓Uses real testimonials, with permission.
  • ✓Real deadline and terms (“through Friday,” “10 spots”).

✗ Copy that damages the brand

  • ✗“Guaranteed results” when you can’t guarantee them.
  • ✗Fake urgency: “last spots” that never run out.
  • ✗A testimonial or number made up by AI.

Try this: generate three versions of a sales text with the same structure.

Follow my brand’s voice guide (it’s in the project instructions).
Product or service: <what it is, the price, and what the customer gets>
For: <the audience, with their main pain point in their own words>
Proof I actually have: <authorized testimonial, real number, or "none yet">
Channel: <Instagram caption, email, WhatsApp message>

Write 3 versions with this structure: hook, problem, solution, proof, and call to action.
Version A: starts with a question. Version B: starts with an everyday scene. Version C: starts with the result.
Rules: don’t make up numbers, testimonials, or timelines; if proof is missing, use the guarantee or process as support.
Mark anything that makes a claim about the product with [CHECK].

How to check: Check each [CHECK] against the actual product. Read the three versions aloud: the one you’d have the courage to say while looking at the customer is the one to publish. If possible, publish two and compare the responses.

Why learn

A clear structure helps short copy sell more than long, confusing copy. And honesty sells for longer: a misled customer won’t come back and will tell others. AI speeds up the writing; you build trust.

Key concepts

Copy
copy that prompts action
Hook

Proof

Call to action

💬 Customer service and sales funnel

Many sales are lost not in the ad, but in the conversation: the question that went unanswered overnight, the quote that took too long, the customer who bought once and never heard from the store again. Here, AI can help in very practical ways—drafting replies, organizing frequently asked questions, and reminding you who needs a follow-up—as long as a person stays in control.

🌱 New here?

Sales funnel is the path someone takes from discovering a brand to buying and buying again; it narrows because not everyone makes it to the end. Lead is an interested contact who hasn’t bought yet. CRM is the system (or spreadsheet) where you record each customer and what still needs to be done for them. Follow-up is the agreed follow-up after a conversation.

What it is

It means using AI at each stage of the funnel—attract, engage, talk, sell, and bring customers back—with clearly defined tasks: drafting replies, creating an FAQ, preparing a proposal, and setting reminders. The person sends the messages and makes the decisions.

📚 Answer library

Paste in the 30 questions you get most often and ask for answers in your tone. Review them one by one and save them as WhatsApp Business quick replies.

🏷️ Sorting

Ask it to sort the day’s messages into "question," "quote," "complaint," and "compliment," and start with the complaints.

🔁 Follow-up

Once a week, paste in the list of open quotes and ask for a follow-up draft for each one.

💡 Tip

Bots that reply on their own in WhatsApp do exist, but start with reviewed drafts. Once your answer library is solid, you can decide what can be automated—in Module 1.7, you’ll see how to set up that workflow safely. Customer conversations are personal data: use accounts with terms that comply with the LGPD (Brazil’s data protection law).

Why learn

Responding quickly and well, and remembering to follow up with someone who asked for a quote, often brings in more sales than posting more. This is where AI saves time without removing the human touch that closes a deal.

Key concepts

Funnel
from first contact to repeat purchase
Lead

Follow-up

Human in control

📊 Measure what matters

Likes feel good, but they don’t pay the bills. To know whether your marketing is working, you need a few numbers tied to sales: how many conversations started, how many quotes went out, how many sales closed and where each one came from. AI is a good partner for reviewing your spreadsheet and spotting patterns—but it can only see what you measure.

What it is

Measuring means choosing three to five indicators tied to your goal, recording them every week in a simple spreadsheet, and asking AI to compare periods, point out what changed, and suggest a test for the following week.

MetricWhat it tells youType
Likes and followersHow many people responded; tells you little about purchases.Vanity
Saves and sharesThe content was useful enough for someone to save or send to another person.Interest
Clicks and messages startedThe person took a step toward you.Intent
Quotes and sales by sourceWhich channel brings in real money.Business

Goal: reviewing the month’s numbers with AI and coming away with a test to try.

[attach the spreadsheet exported from your social networks or paste the table]
My goal this month was: <e.g., get more quote requests through WhatsApp>
Spreadsheet columns: <date, post type, pillar, reach, saves, messages, sales>

1. Calculate from the data and show your work.
2. Compare the pillars and formats: which ones generated the most messages and sales?
3. Separate patterns (show up multiple times) from things that may be coincidence (happened once).
4. Suggest ONE test for the next two weeks, including what to change and how to measure it.
Don’t make up numbers that aren’t in the spreadsheet.

How to check: Manually redo one of the calculations AI showed (for example, the total messages for one pillar). If it doesn’t match, ask it to recalculate using the data analysis tool. The suggested test needs to say exactly what you’ll change.

✓ Good reading of the numbers

  • ✓Compare equal time periods (week to week).
  • ✓Change one thing at a time so you know what worked.

✗ Misleading reading

  • ✗Draw conclusions from a single post that went viral.
  • ✗Accept the AI’s calculations without checking any of them.

Why learn

Without measuring, you repeat what takes effort and abandon what gets results without realizing it. With three or four of the right numbers and AI helping you interpret them, each month teaches you something concrete about your customer.

🚀 Your 7-day campaign

Time to bring everything together in a real workweek. The plan below doesn’t promise a sale in seven days—it promises to learn from your audience in seven days, using everything this module covered: research, voice, calendar, copy, customer service, and measurement. One hour a day is enough.

What it is

A short, complete campaign for a single offer: one day to research, one to prepare your voice and calendar, four days to publish and talk with people, and the last day to measure results and decide what to do next.

Day 1 · Research. Gather real comments and messages and build the customer map (topic 1). Choose ONE offer.

Day 2 · Preparation. Save the voice guide as a standing instruction and generate the week’s plan with the prompt below.

Days 3 and 4 · Teach and show. Publish trust-building and proof posts. Reply to every comment the same day.

Day 5 · Invite. Publish the offer with honest copy and a clear call to action. Have answers to frequently asked questions ready.

Day 6 · Talk. Follow up with people who asked questions but didn’t buy, using messages you’ve reviewed.

Day 7 · Measure. Record messages, quotes, and sales, and use the numbers review prompt (topic 6).

Goal: generate the complete 7-day campaign plan for one offer.

Follow my brand’s voice guide.
This week’s offer: <product or service, actual price, terms, and deadline>
Customer map: <paste their pain points, wants, objections, and where they are>
Channels: <Instagram, WhatsApp, email>. Time available: 1 hour a day.

Build the plan for days 3 through 6 in a table:
Day | Pillar (teach, show, invite) | Format | Hook | Full text | Call to action | Objection it addresses
Then write:
- 5 ready-to-use answers to the most likely questions about the offer;
- 1 polite follow-up message for someone who asked a question and then went quiet.
Don’t make up testimonials, numbers, or deadlines. Mark anything I need to validate with [CHECK].

How to check: Each objection on the customer map needs to be addressed by at least one post or ready-to-use answer. Check every [CHECK]. If the plan takes more than an hour a day, ask to simplify it.

💡 Tip

At the end of the week, write three lines about what you learned about the customer. That learning is worth more than the sales number from the first campaign, and it’s what will make the second one better.

Why learn

Completing the whole cycle once, on a small scale and with good measurement, teaches you more than reading about marketing for months. And the cycle repeats: research, voice, content, engagement, and measurement become your AI-assisted sales routine.
